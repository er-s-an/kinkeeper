What this demo can and cannot do

  • Maggie is fictional; what you type is not. Maggie Rivera is not a real person, and the seeded journal is sample data. Anything you type into this demo, however, is saved in the demo journal on this computer — so please enter only fictional details, never real personal information about anyone.
  • Nothing is ever sent. No push notification, text message, email, or phone call leaves this page. Family cards wait in the Family updates view until someone opens it. “Mark as seen” only changes the card here — it does not message Maggie or confirm anyone is safe.
  • No emergency response. KinKeeper cannot contact emergency services and must never be relied on in an urgent situation.
  • No medical advice. KinKeeper is not a medical device. It repeats what Maggie said in plain words — it does not diagnose, monitor health conditions, or recommend treatment.
  • Statuses describe words, not health. “Checked in” means Maggie sent a message today. “Needs attention” means her own words mentioned something like missed medication — it is a cue to look, not an assessment.

If a family member is concerned

In a real product, the right next step when a card worries you is simple: call or visit your person directly. A card is a reason to reach out yourself — never a substitute for it, and never proof that help is on the way. In this demo, marking a card as seen changes nothing outside this page.

What stays, what fades

Maggie’s journal and Dana’s cards persist on this computer — reload the page and they are still there, which is how KinKeeper recalls earlier days. The visible conversation on the Today view is session-only: it clears on reload, and KinKeeper says so. The demo itself runs locally in one process on this machine — no cloud, no accounts. The optional microphone and voice buttons use your browser’s own speech features; depending on the browser, that audio may be processed by the browser maker rather than staying on this machine.

How one check-in travels the thread

  1. Maggie’s words — exactly what she said, kept in her journal on this computer.
  2. What KinKeeper noticed — plain facts from her words, like “medication: not taken (her words)”. Negations count: “I did not fall” is recorded as ruled out, not as a fall.
  3. Speaking first — when a morning goes quiet, KinKeeper starts the conversation instead of waiting.
  4. A card for Dana — worrying words become an amber card with Maggie’s exact quote, waiting in Family updates. Nothing is sent anywhere.
